01 / THE BASICS

What is a computer network?

A computer network connects devices so they can exchange information and share resources using wired or wireless links.

▣

Devices

Computers, phones, printers, servers and smart devices.

Example: a laptop sends a document to a printer connected to the same network.

⌁

Connection media

Ethernet cables, fibre-optic cables and wireless radio signals.

Example: Wi-Fi links a phone to a router without a physical cable.

⇄

Protocols

Rules that help devices format, address and exchange data.

Example: TCP/IP supports communication across many interconnected networks.

02 / NETWORK FAMILIES

Four scales of connection.

Choose a network type to see how devices connect and where it is used.

PANPERSONAL AREA NETWORK

Your personal space

Connects personal devices over a short distance.

Earbuds
Watch
How it connects: Bluetooth or a personal hotspot links a phone with earbuds, a watch or a laptop. Example: listening to music from a phone through wireless earbuds.
LANLOCAL AREA NETWORK

A room or building

Connects computers and shared resources in a home, lab or office.

PC 01
Switch
PC 02
Printer
How it connects: Ethernet cables or Wi-Fi link devices to a switch or wireless router. Example: a college computer lab sharing a printer and internet access.
MANMETROPOLITAN AREA NETWORK

Across a city

Links separate local networks across a metropolitan area.

Campus A
Office B
Campus C
How it connects: provider infrastructure, often fibre-optic links, joins LANs around a city. Example: connecting multiple campuses in one metropolitan area.
WANWIDE AREA NETWORK

Across the world

Connects networks across regions, countries and continents.

Region A
Region B
How it connects: routers forward packets between distant networks using carrier links, fibre or satellite connections. Example: the internet is the best-known global WAN.
